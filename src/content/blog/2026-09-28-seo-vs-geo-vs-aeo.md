SEO, GEO, and AEO all overlap, but they're not the same thing, and treating them as interchangeable is a common, understandable mistake. Here's the actual difference: SEO targets ranking in a list of links, GEO targets being recommended by an AI model, and AEO targets being the direct answer an AI tool gives someone.

## SEO: getting ranked

Search Engine Optimisation is the original discipline - making sure a page ranks well in traditional search results like Google. It's built around keywords, backlinks, page speed, and the technical health of a site. The goal is a click: someone sees a page in a list of results and chooses to visit it.

## GEO: getting recommended

Generative Engine Optimisation is about a different moment - someone asking an AI tool something like "who's good at X," and that tool naming specific brands in its answer. GEO isn't about ranking in a list; it's about being one of the names that gets said out loud, so to speak. The audience isn't a person scanning search results, it's a model deciding who's worth mentioning.

## AEO: being the answer

Answer Engine Optimisation is about content getting used directly to answer a specific question - "what is X," "how does Y work." Rather than being one of several recommended brands, AEO is about a piece of content itself being pulled out and used as the answer, sometimes with no click and no brand recommendation involved at all, just the information itself.

## Where they overlap

All three depend on a lot of the same underlying work: a technically healthy site, clear and well-structured content, real expertise rather than filler. None of them succeed on trickery - content that's genuinely good at ranking tends to also be reasonably well positioned for GEO and AEO, because the same qualities (clarity, structure, real substance) matter to all three.

## Where they genuinely diverge

The difference shows up in what "winning" looks like:

- **SEO wins** when a page shows up in the top results and gets clicked.
- **GEO wins** when a brand gets named as a recommendation, whether or not anyone visits the site at that moment.
- **AEO wins** when a specific answer gets used, sometimes without the source being named or clicked at all.

That last point is uncomfortable for a lot of businesses: AEO success can mean genuinely useful visibility with zero traffic to show for it. It's still worth having - being the trusted source an AI model draws from builds a different, harder-to-measure kind of authority - but it's a real shift from the click-based way SEO has traditionally been judged.

## Do businesses need all three?

Not necessarily all at once, and not with equal effort. A local service business might care most about GEO (being recommended). A business selling something people research heavily might care most about AEO (answering their questions well). Most businesses benefit from all three working together rather than picking just one - but understanding which one actually matters most for a specific business is a better starting point than trying to do everything equally from day one.
