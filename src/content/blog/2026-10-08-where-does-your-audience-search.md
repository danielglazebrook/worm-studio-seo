Before you can target your audience across SEO, GEO and AEO, you need to know which one they're actually using — and that depends heavily on age, device, and industry, not assumption. Get this step wrong and the rest of the strategy is built on a guess.

## Why you can't assume

The honest, if inconvenient, answer is that "your audience" almost certainly isn't one single behaviour. A few real patterns worth knowing before assuming anything:

- **Age is the biggest single factor.** Pew Research found that 43% of 18–29 year-olds now turn to an AI tool or TikTok *before* Google when looking for recommendations or products — for this group, an AI assistant is becoming the default starting point the way Google was for their parents. That drop-off is steep with age: adoption falls sharply past 45, and again past 55.
- **Device matters more than most businesses expect.** Research consistently shows ChatGPT sees stronger engagement on desktop — consistent with research-heavy, professional use — while Google keeps its dominance on mobile, where casual and on-the-go searching still rules.
- **Industry changes the picture too.** AI platform usage isn't evenly split across sectors — some industries see a meaningfully higher share of traffic from Gemini specifically rather than ChatGPT, tied to how those audiences already use Google's ecosystem day to day.

None of this means AI search "isn't relevant yet" for an older or more traditional audience — Google itself still dominates search overall for every age group. It means the *mix* is different depending on who you're actually trying to reach, and guessing that mix instead of checking it is where a lot of GEO effort gets wasted.

## How to actually check, rather than assume

1. **Look at who your real customers are, not who you imagine them to be.** Pull actual customer or audience age data if you have it — from analytics, from your CRM, from a quick survey. Age is doing more predictive work here than almost anything else.
2. **Check device split in your existing analytics.** If most of your traffic and sales already come from mobile, Google (and AI Overviews within Google) matters more than a standalone AI tool. Heavy desktop usage points the other way.
3. **Run real prompts yourself.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and a plain Google search the actual questions your customers would ask — not generic industry terms, the specific phrasing a real person would use. What comes back, and whether you appear at all, tells you far more than any general statistic.
4. **Watch for industry-specific patterns**, not just demographic ones. A B2B, research-heavy purchase behaves differently to an impulse, mobile-led one, regardless of the buyer's age.

## A real example: getting this wrong ourselves

This isn't hypothetical advice — it's a mistake worth being honest about. Early on, the AI visibility tracking set up for this site was checking prompts like *"Shopify SEO Services and Experts"* and *"Shopify Design and Development UK"* — perfectly reasonable prompts, except they reflected the old positioning of this business, not a question anyone actually asks when looking for what Worm Studio does now. The tracking was technically working. It just wasn't measuring anything useful, because the audience assumption behind it was already out of date.

The fix wasn't a technical one — it was going back to step one: asking what a real prospective client would actually type into ChatGPT or Google today, and rebuilding the tracked prompts and content around that instead. That's the same process described above, just applied to our own site rather than a client's.

## Once you know: how to target each channel

**For SEO** (still the foundation regardless of AI adoption): technical health, page speed, clear site structure, and content that directly answers real search queries — the fundamentals haven't changed, they've just become table stakes rather than the whole strategy.

**For GEO**: focus on being genuinely citable — specific, well-reasoned content an AI model would want to recommend, not generic marketing copy. Confirm your site is actually crawlable by AI bots (a lot of them don't execute JavaScript), and make sure structured data gives models a clean, explicit signal about what you do.

**For AEO**: structure content to answer real questions directly, near the top, in the language your audience actually uses — which is exactly why step 3 above (running real prompts yourself) matters as much for content planning as it does for research.

The order matters: audience research first, channel tactics second. Skipping straight to tactics without checking who's actually there, and where, is the fastest way to optimise carefully for the wrong thing.
